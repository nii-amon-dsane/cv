# Company Research: OpenFn

> Depth: standard
> Date: 2026-10-07

## 1. Snapshot

OpenFn is an open-source Digital Public Good for workflow automation, data exchange, interoperability and AI orchestration. It is designed primarily for governments, NGOs and other impact-oriented organizations that need critical systems to exchange data and automate public-service workflows.

- **What they do:** Open-source workflow automation and integration infrastructure for Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI), including government, health, humanitarian and social-service use cases.
- **Who they serve:** Governments, ministries, NGOs, implementers and international-development organizations.
- **Business model:** Open-source software plus hosted subscriptions, managed deployments, enterprise support, implementation, training and expert services. The current Services strategy is moving from project-shaped consulting toward subscription bundles with recurring Expert Days and support.
- **Headquarters:** Fully distributed / remote organization; no single operational headquarters was material to this role.
- **Founded:** OpenFn traces the product to 2014; founder and CEO Taylor Downs.

OpenFn's current site reports projects in 60+ countries, roughly 5 million workflow runs per year and approximately 50 million records handled annually.

## 4. Tech stack signals

- **Core platform:** OpenFn Lightning.
- **Application stack:** Elixir / Phoenix LiveView with PostgreSQL.
- **Execution/runtime:** Node.js and TypeScript are also part of the platform ecosystem.
- **Deployment:** Docker and Kubernetes are supported for self-hosting/deployment.
- **Integration model:** 70+/100+ open-source adaptors are referenced across current OpenFn materials for APIs, databases, SFTP, files, health systems and other public infrastructure.
- **AI:** Current product positioning includes AI-assisted workflow building/debugging and broader AI orchestration, with an explicit emphasis on government data control and sovereignty.
- **GitHub:** OpenFn/lightning is public and actively maintained.
- **Role implication:** The Managing Director is not primarily a coding role, but technical credibility in integration architecture, APIs, distributed workflows, security and delivery is central.

## 5. Market position

- **Category:** Digital Public Infrastructure, workflow automation, interoperability, integration / iPaaS for the public and social sectors.
- **Direct competitors:** No simple one-for-one competitor. Adjacent options include commercial iPaaS/workflow products, custom system-integrator builds and other interoperability layers used in public infrastructure.
- **Differentiation:** Open source, deploy-anywhere/data-sovereignty model, strong government/NGO implementation experience, public-sector adaptors and workflows, and an implementation-partner ecosystem.
- **Geographic focus:** Global, with strong current activity in Africa, Latin America and public-sector health/social-service programs.
- **Scale:** Current official site reports 60+ countries, ~5M workflow runs annually and ~50M records handled annually.

## 8. Why this role might be open

1. **Business-model transition — explicit:** Services is being separated from end-to-end selling and reshaped into a focused delivery/technical-authority organization within subscription bundles.
2. **Government implementation growth — explicit:** The JD says OpenFn has eight government implementations this year across Africa and Latin America and needs stronger portfolio leadership as adoption accelerates.
3. **Productization and partner scale:** The company is trying to move implementation knowledge from individuals into playbooks, AI tooling, repeatable service products and a wider partner network.

## 9. Strategic hooks for the application

- **National government infrastructure:** GenKey/Ghana Electoral Commission is unusually direct evidence for OpenFn's preference for national-scale government implementations.
- **Digital health + international development:** Living Goods and MFieldwork map directly to OpenFn's health, NGO and public-service customer base.
- **Integration architecture:** Bridge, SafariFone, Radio Africa and GenKey show long-running experience designing multi-system integrations rather than isolated applications.
- **Leadership of managers:** Peach, Bridge, Living Goods and Radio Africa support the requirement to lead a leadership bench rather than only individual contributors.
- **Shared-services operating model:** Radio Africa's 12-person shared-services organization is useful evidence for service-delivery leadership and standardization.
- **AI with deterministic boundaries:** Current AI-native product work maps well to OpenFn's AI orchestration direction without presenting AI as a replacement for reliable transactional workflows.
- **Africa-based executive:** Nairobi is directly aligned with the geographic preference.
