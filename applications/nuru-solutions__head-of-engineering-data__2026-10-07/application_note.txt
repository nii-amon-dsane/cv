The most complex system I have taken into production was part of Ghana's national biometric voter-registration programme ahead of the 2012 election.

At GenKey Africa, I led development work on the Biometric Voter Register Adjudication Client and contributed to the ABIS software used by the Electoral Commission. The programme ultimately registered approximately 14 million voters using 6,500 biometric registration kits.

The difficult part was not simply processing a large volume of records. Biometric matching is probabilistic, so automated matching alone cannot safely resolve every possible duplicate or ambiguous record. The workflow needed a controlled way to separate cases that could be resolved automatically from cases requiring human review without compromising the integrity of the register.

We built an adjudication workflow around that boundary: the ABIS handled automated matching while the adjudication client gave trained operators a structured process for reviewing ambiguous cases. I worked across process modelling, interface development, product delivery, demonstrations and technical discussions with the Electoral Commission and training key trainers.

That project shaped how I think about production data and AI systems today: automate aggressively where confidence is high, make exception paths explicit, keep validation observable and never hide uncertainty behind a model score.
