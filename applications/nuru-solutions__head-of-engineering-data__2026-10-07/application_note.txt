The most complex system I have taken from prototype to production was Radio Africa's music-streaming and catalogue-ingestion platform.

The complexity started at intake. Universal Music Group, Sony, Africori, MCSK and smaller providers all supplied catalogues differently: different formats, quality levels, transport mechanisms and asset completeness. Some supplied artwork and others did not, while our product required complete assets. We therefore built the normalization layer ourselves, including validation, missing-asset detection, quality control and manual / web-assisted backfill. The platform also had to enforce DRM and subscription access, attribute playback accurately for rights reporting and artist payments, and process partner catalogues within agreed availability windows.

The first production design broke when we received our first large catalogue dumps from Sony. We had built ingestion as a single mainline process that generated every media quality sequentially. Large bursts overwhelmed processing capacity, and an FFmpeg failure — often caused by memory pressure — could require the whole item to be processed again.

I re-architected the system around asynchronous Kotlin/Akka processing and RabbitMQ. A main ingestion task fanned out into independently processed quality renditions, and the work was made idempotent so retries only repeated incomplete processing. We also moved suitable workloads from reserved AWS instances to Lambda-based execution, allowing capacity to scale with catalogue demand instead of paying for peak infrastructure continuously.

The redesign improved failure isolation, recoverability, cost and burst scalability, and let us decompose services so Python, Java and later Kotlin could each be used where they fit best. In parallel, I ran an intensive upskilling programme for a young team covering dependency injection, background processing, scalability and idempotency.

That system taught me that production architecture is as much about messy inputs, failure recovery, commercial constraints and team capability as it is about the happy-path design.
